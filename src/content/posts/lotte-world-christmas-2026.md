When Seoul turns freezing in December, outdoor plans start to look a lot less fun. That's when **Lotte World Adventure** (롯데월드 어드벤처) in Jamsil earns its reputation: it's one of the world's biggest indoor theme parks, built under a giant glass dome with an ice rink in the middle, so you can ride coasters and watch parades in a T-shirt while it's well below zero outside.

This winter the park has teamed up with **Cookie Run**, the Korean mobile game whose cookie characters are everywhere in Seoul's character shops. The festival is called **Cookie Run X Lotte World: Sweet Winter Adventure**, and it runs from **November 21, 2026 to March 1, 2027**. The story is simple and very Christmassy: the cookies have just come out of the oven and moved into the park, bringing a daily parade, photo sessions, a winter stage show and a lot of gingerbread-themed decoration.

If you're in Seoul over the holidays with kids, a partner or just an appetite for very Korean theme-park energy, it's one of the easiest festive days out in the city. It's connected to a subway station and runs late into the evening.

> **Heads up:** The photos in this post show Lotte World Adventure on ordinary days in past years, not this year's Cookie Run festival decorations.

The headline event is the **Happy Christmas Parade**, which runs **every day at 2pm and 8pm** along the first-floor parade route. This year GingerBrave, Angel Cookie, Milky Way Cookie and Eternal Sugar Cookie join the floats. Lotte World's parades are known for being big, bright and loud, and the 8pm run is the one to pick if you want the lights at their best.

Find a spot along the route 20–30 minutes early, especially on weekends and over the Christmas holidays. The upper-floor balconies that ring the park are a good fallback if the ground level is packed.

## Cookie Run photo times, show and decorations

Beyond the parade, the festival fills the indoor park with things to stop and photograph:

- **Cookie Run photo time** at Meeting Plaza (만남의 광장), daily at **3:30pm and 5pm**. It's the best chance for kids to get a picture with the characters.
- **A winter stage show**, "The Magic Match and the Dreaming Night", at Garden Stage at **6:30pm**.
- **Meeting Plaza** becomes a snowy "dessert kingdom" with gingerbread houses, cookie characters and a big decorated Christmas tree.
- **Eternal Sugar Cookie's Sweet Paradise**, an interactive zone at Winnie Bini Plaza with a media wall where you scan a QR code to design your own cookie or hang a wish on a digital tree, plus a step-pad game and life-size character standees.
- **A giant GingerBrave balloon** and winter decorations around the ice rink, plus photo frames and booths.

There's also festival food (themed pizza, pasta and drinks tied to the characters) and Cookie Run merchandise such as plush keychains and headbands, released in stages. Spending above set amounts on themed food or merch earns small extras like random stickers, while stocks last.

## Magic Island and the castle after dark

Cross the bridge from the indoor park and you reach **Magic Island**, the outdoor section on Seokchon Lake with the famous **Magic Castle** and the park's biggest thrill rides, including the **Atlantis** coaster, usually the longest queue in the park. After sunset this winter, the castle walls become a screen for a Christmas projection show called **"Starlight Wishes"**. Show times weren't published in the festival announcements, so check the app or the park map on the day.

Keep in mind that Magic Island is fully outdoors. In winter it's cold and windy by the lake, and the operator says outdoor attractions can close in adverse weather. If the big coasters are your priority, go early in the day and check ride status before you head out.

Even without the festival, the indoor park is a lot to take in. It's split into European-style themed streets with half-timbered houses, blue-roofed shops and flag-lined facades, and a monorail loops overhead. Rides run from gentle carousels and balloon rides to dark rides full of dragons, pirates and jungle animals.

## How to get there

Lotte World Adventure is right next to **Jamsil Station** (잠실역), served by **Line 2** (green) and **Line 8** (pink). Follow the signs for **Exit 4** and Lotte World Adventure. The main gate is a few minutes' walk away, and you can get there without spending long outside.

- **From Myeongdong:** take Line 4 to Dongdaemun History & Culture Park, change to Line 2 and ride to Jamsil. Allow roughly 40 minutes.
- **From Hongdae:** Line 2 runs directly from Hongik University Station to Jamsil with no transfer. Allow roughly 50 minutes.

For maps, search **롯데월드 어드벤처** in **Naver Map** or **KakaoMap**. Google Maps in Korea shows subway routes but doesn't give turn-by-turn walking directions, so the Korean apps are much more useful for the last few minutes on foot.

## Tips for international visitors

- **Buy tickets online.** The regular one-day pass is **₩67,000** for adults, ₩58,000 for teens and ₩50,000 for children. Discounted tickets for foreign visitors are widely sold on travel booking platforms, so compare before paying gate price. Some Korean discounts only work with Korean cards or phone plans.
- **Hours vary.** The park usually opens at 10am and closes at 9pm Sunday to Thursday and 10pm on Fridays and Saturdays, with last entry earlier. Confirm your date on the official site.
- **Plan around the queues.** Weekends, Christmas week and Korean school holidays get very busy. Lotte World's app offers ride reservations and shows wait times; check whether your ticket type can be registered.
- **Dress in layers.** Indoors is warm, but Magic Island is outdoors by the lake. Bring a coat you can carry or put in a locker.
- **Make an evening of it.** The **Lotte Town Christmas Market** at the foot of Lotte World Tower is right next door this season (see our [this week in Seoul guide](/posts/things-to-do-in-seoul-this-week-nov-30-dec-6-2026/)). **Seokchon Lake** is good for a walk, and the observation deck at **Lotte World Tower** is close by.

## Is it worth it?

If you love theme parks, are traveling with kids or simply want a warm, festive day out in a Seoul winter, yes. The daily parades, Cookie Run decorations and indoor setting make it an easy pick in cold weather. If you only care about big outdoor coasters, check the forecast first, because Magic Island is at the mercy of the weather. And if you're on a tight budget, book a discounted ticket in advance rather than paying full price at the gate.
