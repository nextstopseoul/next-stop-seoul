If there's one photo almost every first-time visitor to Seoul ends up taking, it's this one: a wall of books curving up toward a glass roof, with tiny people walking along the bottom. That's **Starfield Library** (별마당도서관, *Byeolmadang Doseogwan*), an open-plan "library" in the middle of COEX Mall in Gangnam. It costs nothing to get in, it's open every day of the year, and you can reach it from the subway without stepping outside — which makes it one of the easiest wins on a cold December day.

Winter is also when the library puts on its biggest show. Every year a giant seasonal installation goes up in the central hall, and the Christmas tree here has become a small Seoul tradition. For 2026 the theme is **"K-Christmas"**: from **November 13, 2026 to January 31, 2027**, the centerpiece is a tree inspired by *Irworobongdo* (일월오봉도), the Sun, Moon and Five Peaks painting that once stood behind the king's throne in Joseon-era palaces. Think blue mountain peaks, a sun and moon, cranes and pine trees, plus a white tiger in a Santa hat pulling a gift palanquin — a very Korean take on Christmas.

> **Heads up:** The photos in this guide show the library on ordinary days and, in Photo 13, a previous year's Christmas decoration. This winter's K-Christmas tree looks different.

## The 13-metre bookshelves

<figure>
  <img src="/images/posts/starfield-library-christmas-2026/04.jpg" alt="A towering curved bookshelf reaching toward a glass roof, with visitors taking photos at its base" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Photo 4 · The bookshelves rise about 13 metres from the floor.</figcaption>
</figure>

The library opened in 2017 inside Starfield COEX Mall, the huge underground shopping complex attached to the COEX convention center. Its signature feature is a set of bookshelves about **13 metres tall**, wrapped around the two-level hall like the walls of a canyon. The collection runs to more than 50,000 books and over 400 magazine titles, including plenty of international magazines.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/posts/starfield-library-christmas-2026/05.jpg" alt="View from the upper level of two round bookshelf towers facing each other across a busy wooden floor" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Photo 5 · Looking across the hall from the upper level.</figcaption>
</figure>

Be realistic about what this is: it's a public lounge in a busy mall, not a silent reading room. People come to meet friends, snap photos and rest their feet between shops. The upper shelves are mostly there for the view. That said, you really can pull a book or magazine off a lower shelf and sit down with it at the long tables — there's no library card and no checkout.

## This year's K-Christmas display

<figure>
  <img src="/images/posts/starfield-library-christmas-2026/13.jpg" alt="A tall decorated Christmas tree and large star ornaments in front of the bookshelves, surrounded by crowds" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Photo 13 · A previous year's Christmas decoration — the 2026 tree has a different, Korean-painting design.</figcaption>
</figure>

The 2026 campaign, called *READ THE LIBRARY*, wraps the tree in a short Christmas fantasy story by novelist **Lee Mi-ye**, best known for *The Dallergut Dream Department Store*. The story is available as an e-book in **English**, Korean, Japanese and Chinese — you open it via a QR code and read it on a dedicated reader inside the library, so it's one of the few seasonal displays in Seoul that has proper content for non-Korean speakers.

The organizers have also announced some dated extras: Wednesday workshops (folk-painting ornaments and tree colouring) from November 18 to December 30, limited-edition goods on sale through December 31, a book talk with author **Min Jin Lee** on December 2, and a Christmas concert on December 12. Most programs are run in Korean and some may need advance sign-up, so check Starfield's official channels if you want to join one.

## How to get there

<figure>
  <img src="/images/posts/starfield-library-christmas-2026/08.jpg" alt="A wide view of the library's lower level with shops on the left and bookshelves on the right" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Photo 8 · The library sits in the middle of the COEX Mall corridors.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Samseong Station (Line 2), Exits 5 or 6:** These exits connect to COEX Mall underground. Follow the mall signs for 별마당도서관 / Starfield Library; it sits in the center of the mall.
- **Bongeunsa Station (Line 9), Exit 7:** Comes up by the ASEM Plaza side of COEX. Handy if you're also visiting Bongeunsa Temple or the COEX Aquarium.
- **From Myeongdong:** roughly 40 minutes by subway (Line 4, then change to Line 2 at Dongdaemun History & Culture Park).
- **From Hongdae:** roughly 45–50 minutes on Line 2 from Hongik University Station, no transfer needed.

For directions inside Korea, use **Naver Map** or **KakaoMap** and search **별마당도서관**. Google Maps will show you subway routes here, but it doesn't offer turn-by-turn walking directions in Korea, and the mall's underground corridors are easy to get turned around in.

## Tips for international visitors

- **Go early or late.** Weekday mornings right after the 10:30am opening are the calmest. Weekend afternoons are packed, especially in December.
- **No booking, no ticket.** Just walk in. You don't need a Korean phone number for anything here.
- **Paying for things.** Entry is free; the cafés and shops around the library are mall businesses and take cards — card payment is the norm in Korean malls, but carry a backup card in case one is declined.
- **Dress in layers.** It's warm inside and you can arrive underground from the subway, so you won't need your heaviest coat while you're here.
- **Be considerate.** People do read at the tables, so keep the noise down there and don't block the escalators for photos.
- **Make a half-day of it:** cross the road to **Bongeunsa Temple**, a working Buddhist temple facing COEX with a large standing Buddha statue; browse the rest of COEX Mall; or head over to the COEX Aquarium if you have kids with you. Looking for another free, warm indoor stop? Pair it with the [National Museum of Korea](/posts/national-museum-of-korea-guide/) or [Seoul Botanic Park's greenhouse](/posts/seoul-botanic-park-winter/), and don't miss the [Seoul Lantern Festival](/posts/seoul-lantern-festival-2026/) if you're in town from mid-December.

## Is it worth it?

<figure>
  <img src="/images/posts/starfield-library-christmas-2026/03.jpg" alt="The Starfield Library sign at the top of a curved bookshelf, with visitors on the escalator and floor below" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Photo 3 · The library's name runs across the top of the shelves.</figcaption>
</figure>

As a destination on its own, it's a 30-to-60-minute stop, not a full day. But it's free, central to Gangnam, open late and fully indoors — and between now and the end of January, the Korean-painting Christmas tree and the English e-book story give it something you won't find anywhere else. If you're already near Gangnam or Samseong, it's an easy yes.
