Every December, a stream in the middle of downtown Seoul turns into a river of light. The **Seoul Lantern Festival** fills Cheonggyecheon — a restored stream that runs below street level between office towers — with giant handmade lanterns: palaces, people in hanbok, dragons and schools of glowing fish.

It's free, it's right next to Gwanghwamun and Jonggak, and it runs every evening for seven weeks. If you're in Seoul over Christmas or New Year, it's one of the easiest and prettiest things you can do after dark.

> **Heads up:** the photos in this guide are from past editions. The 2026 artworks haven't been revealed yet — we'll update this page when they are.

## What's new for 2026

This year's theme is *Romance of Seoul: A Symphony of Light*. The route is split into four zones with musical names — **Hanyang Overture**, **Analog Rhapsody**, **Neon Sonata** and **Fantasia of Light**. Hanyang is the old name for Seoul, so expect the walk to move from Joseon-era scenes to retro and neon city vibes.

## What you'll see

The lanterns sit right on the water and hang overhead, so you walk *through* the exhibition rather than past it. Past highlights included life-size palace gates, a royal procession in hanbok and a tunnel of floating fish.

## How to get there

The route runs from **Cheonggye Plaza** to **Samilgyo Bridge**, roughly 1 km downstream.

- **Start at the beginning:** Gwanghwamun Station (Line 5), Exit 5 — then walk toward the big fountain at Cheonggye Plaza.
- **Join midway:** Jonggak Station (Line 1) or Euljiro 1-ga Station (Line 2) put you near the middle of the route.
- **From Myeongdong:** it's about a 15-minute walk north, or one stop on Line 2 from Euljiro 1-ga.

Search **청계광장** (Cheonggye Plaza) in Naver Map or KakaoMap. Google Maps shows subway routes in Korea but can't guide you on foot.

## Tips for international visitors

- **Go on a weekday, early.** Weekend evenings get very busy. Right after 6pm on a weekday is the sweet spot.
- **Dress warmer than you think.** You're walking beside water below street level, and December nights in Seoul often drop below freezing. Gloves help if you want to take photos.
- **Walk downstream.** Starting at Cheonggye Plaza means the zones play in order.
- **Make it an evening:** combine the festival with dinner at [Gwangjang Market](/posts/gwangjang-market-food-guide/) — it's at the far end of Cheonggyecheon, about 15 minutes' walk from Samilgyo.

## Is it worth it?

Absolutely, especially for a first winter trip. It costs nothing, needs no booking, and it's in the most central part of the city. Even if you only have one free evening, you can see the best of it in an hour.
