December starts this week, and Seoul is already in full holiday mode. Department stores have switched on their Christmas displays, the first outdoor Christmas markets are open, and the city's best indoor spaces are dressed up for the season. It's cold, though — early December nights regularly hover around freezing — so this week's list mixes festive evenings out with warm, easy indoor stops.

Below are 11 picks for **Monday, November 30 to Sunday, December 6, 2026**, from free light shows to a market lunch and a palace walk. Where we've already written a detailed guide, you'll find a "Full guide" link.

## Lotte Town Christmas Market (Jamsil)

The biggest Christmas market in town is back on the outdoor lawn plaza at the foot of Lotte World Tower, and this year it takes its cue from a 19th-century British winter market — think wooden stalls, ornaments and warm drinks under the tower's lights. After dark, a "Time Show" is projected onto the Lotte World Mall wall from 6pm, and on weekends there are evening carol and musical performances.

It is extremely popular, so entry is capped per hour. General admission is free but means queuing on site; if you hate lines, the paid Fast Pass is the way to go, though slots for the first half of December were released in November and go fast.

**Quick facts:** Dates Nov 19, 2026 – Jan 3, 2027 · Hours: check the official site (Time Show from 6pm) · Price: free general entry; Fast Pass ₩15,000 (≈US$10–11) · Station: Jamsil (Line 2, Line 8) — follow the signs to Lotte World Mall · Booking: Fast Pass via Lotte Department Store's mall app and Naver Booking

## Shinsegae Myeongdong media facade

The front of Shinsegae's flagship department store in Myeongdong is wrapped in a giant digital screen, and its new Christmas video went live on November 6. It's free, it's right in the shopping district most visitors pass through anyway, and it's best after dark, when the square in front fills up with people taking photos. Pair it with street food in Myeongdong.

**Quick facts:** Dates: running now (end date not announced) · Hours: best after sunset; check the official site · Price: free · Station: Hoehyeon (Line 4) or Myeongdong (Line 4) · Booking: not needed

## H Village at The Hyundai Seoul (Yeouido)

The Hyundai Seoul, the glass-roofed department store in Yeouido, has turned its 5th floor into "H Village," a Christmas village that has become one of the season's most photographed spots. Entry is managed through reservations, with a separate booking channel for foreign visitors and an on-site waiting list. Since details change often, check the store's official channels before you go.

**Quick facts:** Dates: from Nov 10, 2026 (end date not announced) · Hours: check the official site · Price: check the official site · Station: Yeouido (Line 5, Line 9) · Booking: reservation or on-site waiting list

## Christmas market at Oil Tank Culture Park (Mapo)

Oil Tank Culture Park is a former oil depot turned into a cultural park, and on winter weekends it hosts a Christmas market with an Oktoberfest-style twist. It's a good alternative to the crowds in Jamsil and fits nicely into a day around Mangwon or the World Cup Stadium area. This week it opens on Saturday and Sunday.

**Quick facts:** Dates: weekends Nov 28 – Dec 20, plus Dec 24–25 (this week: Dec 5–6) · Hours: 1–7pm · Price: check the official site · Station: World Cup Stadium (Line 6) · Booking: check the official site

## Starfield Library K-Christmas tree (COEX)

COEX Mall's famous wall of books has a Christmas tree and holiday decorations this season, and it's free, open late and reachable without stepping outside. This week's highlight is a book talk with author Min Jin Lee on Wednesday, December 2; most programs are in Korean and may need advance sign-up, so check Starfield's channels.

**Quick facts:** Dates: Nov 13, 2026 – Jan 31, 2027 · Hours: daily 10:30am–10pm · Price: free · Station: Samseong (Line 2), Exit 6 · Booking: not needed for the library

## Seoul Botanic Park winter exhibition

When the wind picks up, Seoul Botanic Park's warm glass greenhouse is the place to be. This winter the tropical zone shows off around 40 orchid species and the Mediterranean zone becomes a Christmas garden of Korean-bred poinsettias. New this week: the free Seed Library next door opens a seeds-and-fruits exhibition from December 1.

**Quick facts:** Dates: Nov 13, 2026 – Jan 31, 2027 · Hours: check the official site (closed some Mondays) · Price: greenhouse ₩5,000 (≈US$3–4); Seed Library free · Station: Magongnaru (Line 9, Line Airport) · Booking: not needed

[Full guide](/posts/seoul-botanic-park-winter/)

## National Museum of Korea

Korea's flagship museum is free, enormous and heated — a perfect cold-day anchor. Don't miss the Room of Quiet Contemplation with its two pensive bodhisattva statues. On Wednesday and Saturday it stays open until 9pm, which makes it a good evening plan too.

**Quick facts:** Dates: open year-round · Hours: Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri, Sun 9:30am–5:30pm; Wed & Sat 9:30am–9pm · Price: permanent galleries free · Station: Ichon (Line 4, Line Gyeongui-Jungang), Exit 2 · English: good signage

[Full guide](/posts/national-museum-of-korea-guide/)

## Gyeongbokgung Palace

Seoul's grandest palace looks especially sharp on a clear, cold winter morning, with the mountains behind it and fewer crowds than in autumn. Winter hours are shorter, so plan for an earlier visit. Wear hanbok and you get in free.

**Quick facts:** Dates: open year-round, closed Tuesdays (Dec 1) · Hours: Nov–Feb 9am–5pm, last entry 4pm · Price: ₩3,000 (≈US$2); free in hanbok · Station: Gyeongbokgung (Line 3), Exit 5 · English: ticket machines with English menus

[Full guide](/posts/gyeongbokgung-palace-winter-guide/)

## Bukchon Hanok Village

A short walk east of the palace, Bukchon's lanes of tiled-roof hanok houses are still a real residential neighborhood, so visiting hours apply in the busiest zone. Go mid-morning, keep your voice down and dress for hills and wind.

**Quick facts:** Dates: year-round · Hours: red zone (Bukchon-ro 11-gil area) tourist visits 10am–5pm · Price: free · Station: Anguk (Line 3), Exit 2 · Booking: not needed

[Full guide](/posts/bukchon-hanok-village-guide/)

## Gwangjang Market

Nothing beats a cold day like a crispy bindaetteok (mung bean pancake) fresh off the griddle with a bowl of makgeolli. Gwangjang's food alley is busy all week; go before noon or mid-afternoon for shorter lines at the famous stalls.

**Quick facts:** Dates: year-round · Hours: shops Mon–Sat 9am–6pm (closed Sun); food alley around 9am–11pm, varies by stall · Price: most dishes ₩5,000–15,000 (≈US$3.50–10.50) · Station: Jongno 5-ga (Line 1), Exit 8 · Payment: cards at most stalls, bring some cash

[Full guide](/posts/gwangjang-market-food-guide/)

## Near Seoul: Paradise City "Alpine Holiday" (Incheon)

If you're flying in or out via Incheon, the Paradise City resort on Yeongjong Island has an Alpine-themed Christmas market and an evening media facade. It's free to visit and a nice way to kill time before a late flight.

**Quick facts:** Dates: Nov 13, 2026 – Jan 3, 2027 · Hours: market 11am–8pm; media facade 6–9pm · Price: free · Getting there: near Incheon International Airport; check the resort's site for shuttle details · Booking: not needed

## Last chance

No major events on this list wrap up this week — most holiday displays run into January. Two dates to keep in mind, though:

- **Lotte Town Christmas Market Fast Pass:** bookings for December 18 – January 3 open on **Monday, December 7 at 2pm** (Korea time). If you're visiting over Christmas, set a reminder.
- **National Museum of Korea** is **closed on Monday, December 7**, so if it's on your list, go this week.

## Rainy-day picks

- **National Museum of Korea** — free, huge and open until 9pm on Wednesday and Saturday.
- **Starfield Library** — connected to the subway, open daily until 10pm, and full of Christmas decorations.
- **Seoul Botanic Park greenhouse** — tropical warmth for ₩5,000, plus a free exhibition in the Seed Library from December 1.

## Tips for this week

- **Tuesday, December 1:** Gyeongbokgung Palace is closed. Visit Bukchon, Gwangjang or the museum instead.
- **Sunday, December 6:** most of Gwangjang Market's shops are closed, although many food stalls keep going — the shopping side is quieter.
- **Jamsil crowds:** the Lotte market caps entry per hour, so weekday evenings are far easier than weekends.
- **Maps:** Google Maps shows transit routes in Korea but not turn-by-turn walking directions. Use Naver Map or KakaoMap.
- **Dress in layers:** nights in early December can dip below freezing, especially at outdoor markets.
